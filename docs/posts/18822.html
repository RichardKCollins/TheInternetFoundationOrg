Conservation of Information. True knowledge is measured by its impact and utility, not by sales

If your Multimodal Language Models are not capable of interacting with all humans to improve spatial reasoning in humans – you have failed society, even if you get lots of investors and prizes from a few.
 
There are 8.2 Billion humans now, and about 5.4 Billion using the Internet. If you cannot keep that constantly in mind and work to that scale now, you are going backwards.
 
The world does not need more gimmicks and apps and “engaging chatters”. It needs tools for sustained lives with dignity, hope and resources for all humans and all related species.
 
Do not work toward enriching a few investors, at the cost of still more gadgets with strings attached. Follow the implications of what you are doing and put more effort into verifying the impact, than on making something that might sell for a few weeks. And hurt many because your ability to design is nowhere sufficient to predict correctly how it will change things.
 
Knowledge is also conserved. If the intent is to change a billion lives, and the designers brains and churned input data do not contain that much true information, then it simply cannot be specific enough in all cases for billions of people.
 
Richard Collins, The Internet Foundation
