Global Open Resources, Internet Best Practices, Building global open communities
 
There is a fine line. If the AI companies would index, cite and link to their sources, that might be sufficient for content creators and groups who want to show their work. They generally want to be engaged with their users and communities.
 
Making it seem like the AI owns or created the information and never citing ANY sources is a big part of the very bad habits all the AI groups use on the Internet. An AI that cannot be audited or verified, cannot really be trusted in global Internet society. (about 5.4 Billion humans)
 
Google and countless sites abstract and link to (and now “AI summarize”) many things, most of them owned by someone. Google uses Wikipedia like it is free, but it is the product of many author working for free, and not properly cited for their work, their value added.
 
The people who teach and share on the Internet process much of the information from books and published sources and write it in their own words. But is an AI that treats the works of authors as its own, any worse than publishers who manipulate ownership for their purposes. All the talk about “open” is more about control and manipulation of sources of information. Which comes down to control and manipulation of humans.
 
Huggingface wholesale promotes tools for using the “free” internet, but the AI groups as a whole are not “giving back” or helping to improve the Internet – they are mostly “scraping and selling”. Not applying their ‘magic methods’ to large problems, to real problems, not chat and toy problems.
 
It is a complex and difficult task to map topics and dynamics on the Internet. Much of these discussion get localized to just United States interests and that is a bad mistake now.
 
The “free internet” is highly biased. Usually toward groups who seek monopoly control of something. After 27 years every day, I can usually trace out the dominant and most important flows and changes. But true AIs (neutral verifiable open sources using traditional databases and LLM algorithms ) seems like a good way to go.
 
No social media site is unbiased. Most sites are highly biased toward their own interests and goals and funding. Rather than chopping off the heads of a few, it might be wiser to see what needs to be done in the world. The big players are all heavily funded and got their money by using computers and networks to grow their empires.
