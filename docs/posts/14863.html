There are several platforms that focus on the recording and sharing of human sounds and speech sounds from many languages. However, they might not be classified as traditional social media sites. Here are a few examples:

  1. Phoible: It’s a database that contains phonetically distinct speech sounds from 2,186 languages, which is about 31% of all known spoken languages. This platform illustrates the diversity of human speech sounds (phonemes) across the world at https://www.city.ac.uk/news-and-events/news/2020/02/speech-sounds-in-the-worlds-languages
  2. City University of London: They have conducted extensive research on the articulatory possibilities of humankind and the diversity of speech sounds in the world’s languages. They have also explored the category of speech sounds known as ‘clicks’, which are only used linguistically in the Khoisan group of languages in Southern Africa.
  3. Voice-based Social Media: These are social media products that use audio as their primary means of communication. They can include live conversation rooms, voice messaging, audio creation and editing tools, podcasts, and more. at https://www.voices.com/blog/guide-to-social-audio/

Please note that while these platforms focus on the study and sharing of human speech sounds, they may not necessarily have the social interaction features commonly associated with social media sites.
