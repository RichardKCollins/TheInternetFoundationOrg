@Victor Ikechukwu Agughasi,
 
I assume this is related to your paper, “ResNet-50 vs VGG-19 vs Training from Scratch: A comparative analysis of the segmentation and classification of Pneumonia from chest x-ray images”
 
So you are writing about “Issues in Validated Machine Learning for Automated Diagnosis Using Affordable Chest X-ray Images”?
 
Talk about the social, economic and human impacts of your research. You do not have to validate it now. Write your goals and issues in your thesis and use that to get your next job or assignment. AIs are NOT to be trusted without a certification and validation process. No matter how smart your machine algorithms, not matter how popular the tool might be, if there is no process for verification in place, it should NOT be used on humans.
 
You might want to recommend “global” (meaning all countries) challenge projects so ALL groups working on machine vision algorithms for diagnosis using chest x-rays can test their algorithms in open challenges. This is very likely happening already, but probably ad hoc and probably “gamed” to benefit a few.  Seasoned practitioners can have a voice in developing “global best practices”, if you help develop “open verifiable network projects” that cannot be gamed by any one group or country.
 
I suggest you throw in the term “sustainable” because if you try to force algorithm methods into traditional human only settings, and do not carefully plan for training and remote verification networks, you will hurt real people. You do not want to be responsible for people dying because your process is so slow or complex it takes too long. I say that because of where you are going to school. Because of your country. Workers can use software to help them in their jobs, humans can also supervise AIs to do their work. And humans and AIs working together can create new global industries so workers can work anywhere and live in a low cost country. If there are verification processes in place to assure quality and trace responsibility.
 
When you write your Thesis send me a copy. Best wishes.
 
Richard Collins, The Internet Foundation
