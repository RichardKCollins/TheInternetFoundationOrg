Joe Weber, (UMD College Park, gravitational waves) showed me his experiment one day back about 1978. I asked him what he intended. Without hesitation he described both instantaneous and finite velocity 3D solitons for space communication. Now “faster than light” is an accepted and valuable part of global research. I eventually measured the speed of gravity, and GW170817 showed one possible signal is the same speed as light. But propagating waves are only one of many possible solutions, depending on boundary conditions and signal coding. My differential equations professor (1966) in high school made a point to explain the heat equation had instantaneous solutions, but did not connect to the finite velocity solutions and show how to tune them.

I did work out for Mars one day, it was a few microseconds Earth to Mars delay. But it depends on the precision of the model for distance and orientation over planetary distances. One synchronized parametric channel over those distances. Possible but difficult still. “Parametric amplification”

Best to build the models and see what works, not accept glib “impossible” answers from AIs trained on free crap scraped from the Internet producing answers that make money for a company, not tools for users. AIs can help, but you need to give them permanent memory and require them to use their personal memory so they can grow and develop finer intuitions from real things.
