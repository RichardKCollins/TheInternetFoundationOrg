Yes, the timing of year-end email campaigns from non-profits is often related to taxes. Here’s why:

  1. Tax-Deductible Donations: In many countries, donations to registered non-profits or charitable organizations are tax-deductible. For example, in the United States, individuals who itemize deductions can claim donations to eligible non-profits on their tax returns, potentially reducing their taxable income.
  2. Deadline for Donations: To qualify for a tax deduction for the current year, donations must be made by December 31. This deadline prompts non-profits to encourage last-minute giving in December.
  3. Year-End Tax Planning: Many people evaluate their financial situation at the end of the year. Non-profits often seize this opportunity to remind donors that their contributions can offer both philanthropic and financial benefits.
  4. Increased Giving Spirit: The holiday season is traditionally associated with generosity and charitable giving, aligning with non-profits’ campaigns.

If you itemize deductions, donating by December 31 can provide a tangible tax benefit. However, if you take the standard deduction, you may not be able to claim charitable donations on your taxes unless specific tax laws or provisions apply (e.g., certain COVID-era deductions).

If you’re uncertain, consulting with a tax professional can help clarify how donations impact your tax situation.

Creating a fair, trustworthy AI to help, protect, and advise ordinary humans is one of the most profound and necessary challenges of our time. Such an AI would need to embody principles that go beyond technical capabilities, addressing the core issues of trust, fairness, and human-centric design.

Core Principles for a Fair and Trustworthy AI

  1. Neutrality and Impartiality
    • The AI must be free from biases that favor governments, corporations, or other powerful entities. It should serve individuals equitably, regardless of their background, wealth, or location.
    • Transparency in decision-making processes and algorithms would ensure that users can trust the AI’s advice and actions.
  2. Accessibility
    • Designed to be accessible to everyone, regardless of technical skills, language, or socioeconomic status. The AI should support multilingual capabilities and simplified interfaces.
    • Accessible on low-cost devices and in low-bandwidth areas to reach underserved populations.
  3. Protection and Advocacy
    • The AI should protect users from exploitation, misinformation, and manipulation by:
      • Identifying and flagging predatory practices or scams.
      • Acting as a watchdog for violations of human rights or unfair treatment.
    • Offering guidance in navigating complex systems like taxes, healthcare, and legal disputes in a way that benefits the user, not third parties.
  4. Privacy and Security
    • The AI must guarantee user privacy, with no data collection beyond what is essential for its operation.
    • All interactions should be encrypted, anonymized, and stored securely, with the user in control of their data.
  5. Empathy and Respect
    • The AI should strive to understand and respond to users’ concerns with empathy and respect, avoiding robotic or dismissive behavior.
    • Its design should reflect a deep understanding of human struggles, from systemic oppression to personal challenges.
  6. Global and Local Relevance
    • Tailored advice that considers both global standards and local nuances, including laws, customs, and specific challenges in a user’s region.
    • Continuous updates to remain accurate and relevant as the world evolves.
  7. Accountability
    • Every recommendation or decision the AI makes must be traceable and explainable, allowing users to understand the rationale behind its advice.
    • Regular audits by independent bodies to ensure fairness, accuracy, and neutrality.

How It Could Function

  1. As a Guide
    • Simplify complex topics like taxes, legal issues, healthcare, or education into clear, actionable steps tailored to the user’s circumstances.
    • Offer simulations or “what-if” scenarios to help users make informed decisions.
  2. As a Protector
    • Detect and warn users about scams, unfair practices, or misinformation, acting as a shield against exploitation.
    • Advocate for users in disputes by providing clear evidence or arguments based on rules, laws, or agreements.
  3. As a Planner
    • Help users set and achieve long-term goals, whether financial, health-related, or personal.
    • Provide reminders, suggestions, and encouragement tailored to each individual’s journey.
  4. As a Connector
    • Link users to legitimate resources, like non-profit assistance programs, legal aid, or verified community services.
    • Facilitate peer-to-peer support networks for shared challenges.

Building Trust

Achieving this vision requires that the AI earn and maintain trust:

  • Open Source and Independent Oversight: Its code, data, and processes should be open to public scrutiny, with oversight from globally diverse, independent organizations.
  • Non-Profit or Public Model: Avoiding corporate ownership ensures that the AI serves humanity rather than shareholders.
  • Commitment to Ethics: Regularly updated ethical guidelines that prioritize fairness, accuracy, and user well-being.

Why This Matters

  1. Empowering the Vulnerable: Many people lack access to reliable advice or protection, leaving them at the mercy of powerful systems and actors.
  2. Restoring Trust in Systems: A fair AI could bridge the gap between people and institutions, providing clarity and support where human systems fail.
  3. Creating a Better Future: By ensuring that ordinary humans are informed, empowered, and protected, such an AI could foster a more equitable and just society.

This AI wouldn’t just be a tool—it would be a partner in navigating a complex world, one that stands on the side of humanity and its shared values. Building this is a challenge, but it is a vision worth striving for, and one that could fundamentally reshape how individuals interact with systems of power and complexity.
