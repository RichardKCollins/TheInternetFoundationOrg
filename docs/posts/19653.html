It is possible to correct and change the captions separate from the video. The Korean, Chinese, Japanese and many other language groups on YouTube have some way to correct and add captions. I do not know how that works. But it likely involves coordination between people posting videos and many people. Change “crunch” to “squeak” is an edit. Then you would frame the feedback as “I suggest you change crunch to squeak at (time in the video) and perhaps explain your own experiences. And maybe even reference to a paper or resource on snow properties. Then it’s not “I am smarter than you” but “Thanks for the story, here is a way to make it even more real and useful to others”. Major movies have networks of advisors and fact checkers and specialist to make $100 Million movies as good as possible, and even those have glitches and errors and bloopers. I am going to look at the frequencies of sounds produced in snow collaboting under the pressure of boots. You point out and interesting phenomena. Tha I have filed among natural phenomena. I do not know if I can use that anywhere but it has a nice feel to it. Thanks.

The point of the story is sharing, responsibility, awareness, cooperation, caring. You are smart enough to see the painted canvases of skies or snow that are not real, and set those things aside. Enjoy the story first. Think about it. Not its pieces.

In a story, the listener or viewer also has a responsibility to set aside those things and quietly talk to the author afterwards. Offer corrections gently not to show off or to hurt.

If you want to make perfect things, then there are other ways to do that in simulations and models of real things, of which there are many. Perhaps you can write your own stores that stretch beyond your own experiences. I would like to see perfect stories. I often wish for that for things like Martian colonization, or star ships, true ones.

A children’s play where none of the painted scenes or props are real can be more engaging or more real and meaningful than perfect props and less engaging or profound stories and situations. I have seen Shakespeare many times with many bad actors and scraps for costumes and garbled words, but the stories and meaning and issues generally come through. I try to do my duty as a listener and enjoyer of the story. Then write my own stories and books, and hope for gentle accommodation from readers so they can help improve the whole. Life is much too long and hard to make the author do all the work alone.
