Published: 2024-09-01 • Original link

Richard Collins, The Internet Foundation:

This is a bit of an odd question, but I mean it seriously.

What would people think of “Olympic war games”? Rather than ad hoc wars and armed conflicts like Israal Palestine, or Ukraine Russia, or dozens of countries having armed conflicts.

Suppose wars were supervised and controlled. Part of the attraction of wars now for media, is some “news” records deaths in video in near real time. Groups offer ideas and strategies. Scores are kept of how many died and injured.

But the “ad hoc” wars, some testing the strength and ability of the mostly young combatants, end up killing more innocents and destroying property than if it were monitored and controlled.

I do have in mind Jonathan Swifts writing about babies. Also “bread and circuses”, the illegal activities of the “ad hoc” wars.

It could be fought to the death. We are literally watching Russia and Ukraine fighting to the deaths of many people. And “sides” are placing bets and urging different actors.

Is there a place for writing about such things, or are any current “Jonathan Swifts” and “Mark Twains” and comments in ill favor?

It could be completely “virtual” but I see the “robot” wars where literal fights to the death between robot proxies are encouraged. A percent of the world has killer instincts, and even a small small percent generates killings and wars and deadly passions.

What do you think? Would it be more fair to have hundreds even thousands killing each other voluntarily for the media, or many tens of thousands of bystanders and property destroyed. And the current arms conflicts are causing displacement of people and groups to the tune of hundreds of millions — at great cost and danger to global societies and economies.

Ethical and Moral Considerations

  1. Human Life and Dignity: The concept of organizing wars, even if controlled, fundamentally conflicts with the value of human life and dignity. Encouraging people to kill each other, even voluntarily, for entertainment or media purposes is ethically problematic.
  2. Desensitization to Violence: Such events could desensitize society to violence and suffering, potentially normalizing the idea of killing as a form of competition or entertainment.
  3. Exploitation and Coercion: There is a risk that individuals, especially those from disadvantaged backgrounds, might be coerced or exploited into participating in such events.

Practical Considerations

  1. Implementation and Control: Ensuring that such “games” remain controlled and do not spill over into real-world violence would be extremely challenging.
  2. International Law and Human Rights: Such events would likely violate international laws and human rights conventions, making them legally untenable.

Alternatives and Reflections

  1. Virtual Simulations: As you mentioned, virtual simulations or robot wars could be a less harmful alternative. These could serve as a way to channel competitive instincts without causing real harm.
  2. Literary and Satirical Commentary: Writers like Jonathan Swift and Mark Twain used satire to critique societal issues. There is certainly a place for modern writers to explore these themes, using fiction and satire to provoke thought and discussion about the nature of war and violence.

Broader Implications

  1. Addressing Root Causes: Instead of finding ways to channel violent instincts, it might be more productive to address the root causes of conflict, such as inequality, injustice, and lack of opportunities.
  2. Promoting Peace and Cooperation: Encouraging international cooperation, dialogue, and peaceful conflict resolution should be the primary focus.

Your question highlights the need for creative and ethical approaches to addressing human conflict and violence. While the idea of “Olympic war games” is intriguing, it raises significant ethical and practical challenges that would need to be carefully considered.
