Estimate Direction of a live webcam from position of the moon at known time

Published: 2021-08-25 • Original link

I was looking at https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9k_sg8rhsgk which is on the roof of the Lake Superior Maritime Visitor Center in Duluth, Minnesota at about 11:30 pm.  The moon was shining brightly on the water.

From Google maps I found the satellite view, a rough idea of where the camera was pointing, and the latitude and longitude in decimal degrees.

https://www.google.com/maps/place/Lake+Superior+Marine+Museum/@46.7796017,-92.0922983,623m/data=!3m1!1e3!4m5!3m4!1s0x0:0x9f9b5f3ad6c9e702!8m2!3d46.779847!4d-92.092464

I found “MoonCalc” at MoonCalc.Org and set the location (left side about 2/3rd “set lat/lon”) and used the slider at the top to see the time.  It gave me this URL to improve things later.  I see the decimal lat long, the date and time.  Not sure about “/1/3”.

https://www.mooncalc.org/#/46.6832,-91.9517,3/2021.08.24/22:31/1/3

I took a screen shot to record, since this is live, and I wanted to get the pixel location of the moon relative to the bottom center of the image (1920 * 1080 image is at 960, 1080)

MoonCalc gives me 6.61 degrees altitude and 101.39 degrees azimuth. That is clockwise from North, and on their map it shows nicely.  So (101.39 – 90) = 11.29 degrees south of Eastward, and there is a line to show me when the moon would rise, but it was cloudy there and then, so not available to check.  Looks close.

Editing the screen shot in Windows Paint, I chose the pencil so I could see the x,y coordinates.  The pencil point is easier to see.  Center of the moon is about (1553, 145) pixels in top = 0, and (1553, 1080-145 = 935) in bottom = 0.  I zoomed in a few times to make it easier to center.

The horizon is a bit hard to see at night, but the moonlight helps.  I think it is about (1080-350 = 730) pixels from the bottom.  And (1553-960 = 593) pixels along the horizon to underneath the moon.  That angle is arctan(593/730) = atan(0.8123288) = 39.08 degree. But too tired to work out the real distances from the image.

It looks like the camera is pointing north of east, from the satellite view. I clipped the satellite image and put it in Paint. The center of the daytime image is near the end of the pier to the left of the light house.  It is tedious to match the camera image with the satellite view. Different times and shadows.  I think someone moved the trash can.  There is a white box on the roof. I think that is the camera.

I extended a line horizontally from the camera box (write the number down!) and then one vertically there to the end of the pier where I think the center goes.  That is arctan(126/1063) = 6.77 degree north of east.

I think all cameras should have their camera, its parameters, location and direction published – if they are a public site.  If it is on the Internet it is public.

And the elevation of  the moon above the horizon in the image is about (935-730 = 205) pixels.
